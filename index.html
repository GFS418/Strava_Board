A single unlabeled CSV — no summaries, just 40,000+ tracked points — reconstructed into a portrait of who this athlete is, how hard they train, and whether their effort is efficient. Three basic visual techniques (histogram, scatter, box/violin) plus one advanced technique (a 2-D aerobic-decoupling density map).

Basic techniques
Histogram

This is a night runner

Distribution of run start times across the 24-hour clock.

Insight: Strikingly unimodal at 21:00 with a heavy 8pm–midnight block and an after-midnight cluster — training built around a night schedule, consistent with a college-town routine.
Scatter

Speed comes from stride, not turnover

Cadence vs. speed, colored by heart rate, against the "180 spm" coaching rule.

Insight: Cadence rises only gently and mostly sits below the mythical 180 spm. This runner adds speed primarily through stride length — the folk-wisdom rule simply doesn't describe them.
Box + Violin

Tight weekdays, wide-open weekends

Pace distribution by day of week — medians and full shape together.

Insight: Weekday medians cluster tightly (habitual training) while weekend violins fan out — short fast efforts mixed with long slow ones, the classic fingerprint of a structured plan.
Advanced technique
2-D Density Map

Aerobic decoupling: efficiency holds, then fades

Hexbin of efficiency (speed ÷ heart rate) vs. run progress, with a fitted median drift curve.

Insight: The dense core holds right on baseline for the first ~60% of each run, then bends downward late. A modest late decouple like this signals good — but not elite — aerobic durability; the gain available is extending the point where efficiency starts to fade.
Bonus: Stryd power subset
Scatter + Fit

More bounce, more wasted power

Form power vs. vertical oscillation on the 10 power-equipped runs — a physics sanity check.

Insight: The positive correlation confirms the data is physically consistent: more vertical bounce means more wasted (form) power. The actionable lever — reducing bounce reclaims watts for forward speed.
Verdict

From an unlabeled 40k-row dump, a coherent portrait: a night runner in Ann Arbor training through summer 2019, who gets speed from stride length not turnover, follows a structured weekly plan, holds efficiency steady before a modest late decouple, and — where power data exists — wastes watts to vertical bounce. A busted myth (180 spm), a durability diagnosis, and one concrete efficiency tip.
